## Work Description

*Vibrating Medium: Archaeology of the Pagoda* explores humanity’s enduring aspirations for well-being, salvation, and freedom through three vertical symbols: Dabotap Pagoda, church spires, and the Statue of Liberty. These historical monuments are transformed into fluid particle images that disperse, converge, ascend, and dissolve in response to Buddhist wooden percussion, Gregorian chants, and democratic speeches.

Vertically stacked CRT monitors form a tower-like structure, displaying interconnected sections of each symbolic image. Their distinctive screen texture, low resolution, and physical depth bring obsolete display technology into dialogue with contemporary digital imagery. As the monuments continuously dissolve and reassemble through sound-responsive movements, viewers encounter familiar historical symbols as unstable and evolving visual forms.

The installation invites reflection on how enduring human aspirations can be reinterpreted through changing media technologies, transforming fixed monuments into dynamic images that connect the past with the present.

## Media Technology and Production

The work combines generative AI, 3D modeling, and TouchDesigner to reconstruct historical monuments as dynamic particle imagery. AI-generated visual references inform the creation of 3D models, which are transformed into particle structures using GPU-based Geometry Instancing. TouchDesigner analyzes sound intensity, rhythm, frequency, duration, and interruptions through CHOP operators, translating these signals into particle density, direction, speed, dispersion, and convergence. Through this process, fixed historical icons are continuously reconstructed as sound-responsive moving images.

## Presentation and Experience

The installation features vertically stacked CRT monitors displaying interconnected particle imagery as a single tower-like structure. Viewers experience historical symbols dissolving and reforming in response to sound, encountering the interplay between obsolete display technology and contemporary digital imagery. For online exhibitions, the work can be presented as a video documentation of the installation, preserving its visual and auditory elements.
