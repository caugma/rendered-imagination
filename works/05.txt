## Work Description

The Divide is the third part of the exhibition Layers, which follows the making and growth of life across three spaces. This part turns to life at a microscopic scale, showing cells as they divide and multiply. The imagery takes the form of cells rendered like stained glass, shown in a darkened room. Mirrors on the front and back reflect the projected footage again and again, so that a single division seems to repeat without end and the viewer stands at its center. The work is not a video played in a fixed order. It responds to where the viewer stands, so each visitor meets the division differently. By overlaying the biological process of cell division with the endless reflection of mirrors, the work turns proliferation into a spatial experience.

## Media Technology and Production

Cell stills were generated with GPT Image 2, then turned into video clips with MiniMax Hailuo H3 and Seedance, using start and end frames to control each clip's motion. The clips were edited into the final footage in Adobe Premiere Pro and After Effects. A Hokuyo UST-10LX laser scanner reads the viewer's distance and ties it to how the footage unfolds. Projectors cast the image onto the two side walls, and mirrors extend it.

## Presentation and Experience

The Divide is installed in a darkened gallery, with projectors casting the footage onto the two side walls and mirrors set at the front and back. A Hokuyo laser scanner tracks the viewer's position, and the footage responds as they move. For online viewing, viewers can watch the original footage itself.
